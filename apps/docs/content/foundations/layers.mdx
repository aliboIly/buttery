A layer is a band of the page's stacking order, such as `dropdown`, `modal` or `toast`, with a container that covers the window. Use the layer primitives for anything that floats over the page, rather than a `z-index` of your own, so a menu inside a dialog still opens above the dialog and a toast stays above both.

## The bands

From the lowest. Each band is a token, `--bt-z-<band>`, and buttery's stylelint config fails a `z-index` that isn't one.

<LayerTokens />

## Put something in a layer

`Layer` renders its children above the page in the band you name, in a container that covers the window but lets clicks through. A layer opened from inside another one stacks above it. Menus, popovers, dialogs and tooltips open their own layers, so most code never renders `Layer` itself.

## Scrims

`Scrim` covers the window behind a layer's content: `dim` darkens it, as behind a dialog, and `blur` softens it, as behind a menu that opens over busy content. Put it first inside the `Layer`, so it sits under what it sets apart.

## Sticky bars

`Sticky` keeps a bar at the top or bottom of its scroll container while the rest scrolls, and stacks with the other sticky bars there instead of covering them. `variant="aside"` makes a sticky side column, such as this site's sidebar.

## Floating lanes

Toasts, notices and guides that float over the page go in a lane, so they stack instead of covering each other: toasts at the bottom center, notices at the top end, and guidance, such as a setup guide, at the bottom end. Lanes move out of the way of sticky bars and anything registered with `useFloatingObstacle`. Render into one with `FloatingLanes.Item`; for toasts, call `toast()` from `@buttery/react`.
