Every buttery component works with a keyboard and a screen reader, keeps text readable, and gives every target room to press. This guide lists what that covers and the few things an app still has to do itself.

## What the components do

- Keyboard: every control can be reached with Tab and used with the keys people expect; menus, dialogs and selects follow Radix's patterns. Each component page has a keyboard table.
- Focus: a visible ring on `:focus-visible`, focus moved into a dialog or panel as it opens and back to what opened it as it closes.
- Names: icon-only controls require a `label`; dialogs warn in development when they have no name.
- Contrast: text and icons meet WCAG AA on the surfaces they're meant for, in light and dark.
- Targets: every press target is at least 24 by 24, and standalone buttons at least 28 tall.
- Motion: everything follows the reduced motion setting.
- Forced colors: switches and checkboxes draw themselves in system colors when Windows' high contrast is on, so their state stays visible.

## What's left to you

- Give every page one `h1` and headings in order, and every form field a label.
- Write link and button text that makes sense on its own: "Download the invoice", not "Click here".
- Give images that carry meaning alternative text, and an icon that stands alone a `label`.
- Don't disable a button the person just pressed: use `aria-disabled`, which keeps focus on it.
- Check with a keyboard and a screen reader as you build. `expectButtery` runs axe's rules on every check, but no automated check finds everything.
