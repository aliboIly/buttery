Every buttery component works with a keyboard and a screen reader, keeps text readable, and gives every target room to press. This guide lists what that covers and the few things an app still has to do itself.

## What the components do

<Cards columns={2}>
<Card title="Keyboard">

Every control can be reached with Tab and used with the keys people expect. Menus, dialogs and selects follow Radix's patterns, and each component page has a keyboard table.

</Card>
<Card title="Focus">

`:focus-visible` shows a focus ring in the accent color. Focus moves into a dialog or panel as it opens, and back to what opened it as it closes.

</Card>
<Card title="Names">

Icon-only controls require a `label`, and dialogs warn in development when they have no name.

</Card>
<Card title="Contrast">

Text and icons meet WCAG AA on the surfaces they're meant for, in light and dark.

</Card>
<Card title="Targets">

Every press target is at least 24 by 24, and standalone buttons are at least 28 tall.

</Card>
<Card title="Reduced motion and high contrast">

Everything follows the reduced motion setting. With Windows' high contrast on, switches and checkboxes draw themselves in system colors, so their state stays visible.

</Card>
</Cards>

## What's left to you

<Rules>
<Rule title="Structure every page">

Give every page one `h1` and headings in order, and every form field a label.

</Rule>
<Rule title="Name things plainly">

Write link and button text that makes sense on its own: "Download the invoice", not "Click here". Give images that carry meaning alternative text, and an icon that stands alone a `label`.

</Rule>
<Rule title="Keep focus on a pressed button">

Don't disable a button the person just pressed: use `aria-disabled`, which keeps focus on it.

</Rule>
<Rule title="Check by hand">

Check with a keyboard and a screen reader as you build. `expectButtery` runs axe's rules on every check, but no automated check finds everything.

</Rule>
</Rules>
