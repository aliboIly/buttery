The inspector pattern is the set of parts an editor is made of: a `HeaderBar`, the `Stage` the work sits on, and the `Inspector` of settings beside it. Put them in `AppShell` and an editor works from a 390px phone, where the inspector becomes a sheet, to a wide desktop.

## How the parts fit

<Cards columns={2}>
<Card icon="monitor" title="The header">

`HeaderBar` holds the app's mark, the document's title and its actions. The title truncates when space runs out, and the actions fold into a "More" menu when they don't fit.

</Card>
<Card icon="palette" title="The stage">

`Stage` fits the artwork at its shape into the space it has. Artwork may overlap the canvas's edges, which clip it, and a crop mode puts handles on its frame to resize it.

</Card>
<Card icon="wrench" title="The inspector">

`Inspector` is a panel of titled sections, each an `Inspector.Section`, holding rows of controls. A section can offer Reset for its own settings.

</Card>
<Card icon="template" title="Its rows">

Each `Inspector.Row` is 50px tall, with the label at the start and the control at the end. A slider labels itself with its value badge.

</Card>
</Cards>
